# Battlecard format

The top half is what a rep reads in sixty seconds before a call. The bottom half is reference.

## Above the fold

**Positioning statement.** Exactly two sentences. The first contrasts how they position with how we do.
The second says when we win.

> Watchpost sells safety teams a video platform; Keelstone gives the safety manager, the CFO and the
> broker one shared risk story that shows up at renewal. When the deal will be decided by the people who
> pay for workers' comp, not only the people who watch the video, we win.

**Quick look.** One row per capability from the category's list, in order.

| Verdict | Means |
|---|---|
| `win` | We're clearly stronger |
| `parity` | Close, or we don't know yet ("Not yet assessed.") |
| `loss` | They're clearly stronger |

Each row gets a note under 15 words saying why.

**Why we win.** Three points, each with a proof point a rep can repeat.

**Where they win.** Two to four honest items.

**Objection handling.** Three to five pairs: what they'll say, what you say. Answers redirect to a
question or a proof point rather than arguing.

**Questions to ask the prospect.** Three to five. Each exposes a gap in their offer without naming them.

**Field intel.** Approved rep submissions, newest first, credited, with the source.

## Below the fold

- **What they say:** their current messaging points, plus recent changes.
- **Product lineup:** name and a short description.
- **Compliance, security and privacy claims:** area, their claim, our position.
- **What customers say:** ratings per review site, then positive and negative themes with mention counts.
- **Employee sentiment** (admin only by default): rating, CEO approval, would-recommend, themes.
- **Company snapshot:** founded, HQ, employees, funding, leadership, news.
- **Approved sources:** what the tracker may use, how, and who approved it.

## What to leave out

Long company histories and SWOT grids. Reps don't read them, and the snapshot covers what matters.
